# Positioning Strategy: Headcount Zero

Teimur Gasanov. Revised around solo operator positioning, not performance engineering.

---

## TL;DR

**Positioning:** Headcount Zero. A company with no employees, where every function that would normally be a hire gets built instead, by a senior engineer who builds agents for a living.

**Why it works:** It is a metric rather than an adjective, it contains an inversion (scale by building, not hiring), and it turns every business function into a content slot. It uses your agent expertise as the mechanism instead of the topic.

**The four pillars:** Replaced a Hire. Numbers From a Company of One. Things Ten Years of Engineering Didn't Teach Me. Nights and Weekends.

**Platform order:** Fix LinkedIn now. Build X as the growth engine. Launch Russian-language Telegram as a regional moat. Start Substack last, own it forever.

**The apps are the lab, not the product.** Interviewium and Wait, Professor are case studies your audience reads about. They get their own consumer funnels. What your audience eventually buys is the agent systems you built to run the company.

---

## 1. The positioning

### What you are not

"Solo engineer running apps" is the indie dev category. It is saturated and you already know it. "Agentic Indie" combines the two most generic words available in 2026: "agentic" is on every conference banner and product page, "indie" is the default label for the entire space. Two generic terms do not add up to one distinctive one.

"Impact Engineer" should be retired. It is vague, self-assigned, and tells a reader nothing.

### The Kill Test

If a competitor could say the same sentence about themselves, it is not ownable.

- "I'm a solo dev shipping apps." Thousands can say this. Fails.
- "I'm an agentic indie hacker." Thousands can say this. Fails.
- "I run a company with zero employees where every role is a system I built myself, and I do it nights and weekends while working at an AI company." Almost nobody can say this. Passes.

### The framing: Headcount Zero

You are running a company with no employees. Every function that a normal business hires for gets built instead: growth, ASO, analytics, support, ad creative, competitive research, customer comms. You do not buy those tools or fake them. You build them, with agents, because that is your actual profession.

Most people in the indie space are generalists or marketers who learned to code. You are the inverse: a 10-year senior engineer with agent architecture as the day job, who is deliberately bad at the business side and learning it in public. That combination is rare and defensible.

The one idea you own: **the bottleneck was never the engineering.** Ten years of getting excellent at the thing that turns out not to be what determines whether a product lives. Every piece of content is some version of that discovery.

### Runners-up

- **Department of One.** Same idea, softer and more relatable, less memorable.
- **The Missing Team.** Each post is one role you replaced. Good series name, weaker as a handle.
- **Ship Alone.** Cleaner, but drifts back toward generic indie territory.
- **Engineering the Business.** The closest analog to your friend's Reverse Engineering Marketing, which is exactly why to avoid it. You would read as derivative rather than parallel.

If you want to keep "Agentic Indie" as the handle for continuity, keep it and run Headcount Zero as the recurring series inside it. If you are picking one thing to build on, pick Headcount Zero.

### Supporting layers

- **Voice: benchmarks and receipts.** Every claim gets a number. This is already your instinct and it is the antidote to the vague, moneyless content that dominates the indie space.
- **Story: Bishkek.** Not a method, but the thing that makes you memorable. A solo operator in Central Asia building for a global market is a specific and underused vantage point. Use it as background texture, not as the headline.

## 2. The content engine

One real artifact per week. Something you actually built or actually measured. Everything else is atomization of that artifact.

### Pillar 1: Replaced a Hire

The flagship format. One business function, the agent or system you built to do it, what it cost to build, whether it beat doing the thing manually. Failures included and preferred.

Example slots: App Store review triage, ASO keyword research, ad creative generation and testing, churn cohort analysis, competitor teardown, support inbox, screenshot generation, localization.

### Pillar 2: Numbers From a Company of One

Real downloads, real ad spend, real revenue, real churn, real CAC. Uncomfortable specificity is the entire value here. Most indie content goes vague the moment money enters the conversation. Being the person who does not is a position by itself.

### Pillar 3: Things Ten Years of Engineering Didn't Teach Me

The sell-side learning curve, in public. Pricing, positioning, App Store rejections, ad creative that flopped, an onboarding flow that made sense to you and to nobody else.

This is your highest-trust format precisely because you are not the expert in it. Do not skip it out of discomfort. It is the reason people follow rather than just read.

### Pillar 4: Nights and Weekends

You have a full-time job at Speechify. Do not hide this or let people assume you are full-time indie. The constraint is the interesting part and it is honest. Time-boxed solo shipping alongside a demanding job is a specific, underserved story, and it inoculates you against the "quit your job and go all in" genre that most readers cannot act on anyway.

### Weekly rhythm

| Output | Volume | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Deep artifact | 1 per week | The build itself |
| LinkedIn posts | 3 to 4 | Atomized from the artifact |
| X posts and 1 thread | 5 to 7 | Atomized from the artifact |
| Telegram posts | 2 to 3 | Atomized, Russian |
| Substack essay | 1 per month | Four artifacts rolled up |

Net new writing stays small because everything traces back to one build.

## 3. Platform strategy

Audience size is your stated top priority, so this section is ordered by contribution to that goal.

### X: the growth engine

Discovery for small accounts on X comes overwhelmingly from replies, which carry the highest engagement weight. Broadcasting to 0 followers does nothing. Replying substantively to rising posts from established accounts does.

The play: build a list of 15 to 25 active accounts in indie, AI, and app-building. Reply early, while posts are still climbing, with something specific rather than agreeable. One thread per week from your artifact. Native video is favored and underused.

Target: 25 substantive replies per week. If no reply out-reaches your own posts within three weeks, your target list is wrong.

### LinkedIn: fix what is already working

You have 1,400 followers and 500k impressions over six months. That is real reach on a bad foundation.

The 2026 algorithm runs on an interest graph that rewards dwell time, saves, and comment quality, and it has authenticity measures that suppress generic AI content. Independent sampling by Originality.ai found that likely-AI long-form posts went from roughly half of their late-2024 sample to over three quarters by mid-2026, and that AI-generated posts received around 45% less engagement than human-authored ones. LinkedIn is rolling out a "Seems like AI slop" report option. Commodity news aggregation is the most exposed content type on the platform right now.

Also: external links in the post body cut reach materially. Van der Blom's 2026 report puts the median reduction near 19%; consultants quote higher. Put links in comments and expect those to be throttled too.

Best format for you: document carousels breaking down a Replaced a Hire build. They generate the most dwell time and they suit numbers-heavy content.

Cadence: 3 to 4 posts per week, inside 2 or 3 consistent topic areas so the algorithm can build topic authority around you.

### Telegram: your regional moat

Telegram skews heavily technical. GWI data puts software developers and web developers as the two highest-indexing occupations on the platform. In the Central Asian and Russian-speaking tech ecosystem it is the default channel, not a secondary one.

Run it in Russian, or Russian-first bilingual. This is where your native-language advantage is largest and competition is thinnest. Keep LinkedIn, X, and Substack in English for global reach and career optionality.

Telegram has no algorithmic feed, so growth comes from cross-promotion and consistency rather than discovery. The upside is high click-through and a high-signal audience that tolerates dense, direct technical writing and rewards utility over motivation.

### Substack: own it, start last

Substack's growth engine in 2026 is internal: Notes, Recommendations, and cross-promotion with peer writers. Gergely Orosz grew The Pragmatic Engineer past a million readers on organic word of mouth and Substack's recommendations feature with no advertising.

Do not launch cold. Launch once LinkedIn and X are producing artifacts and you have 500 existing subscribers to seed from. Free tier only for now. This is the asset no algorithm can take from you, which is why it comes last and stays forever.

## 4. The LinkedIn pipeline

**Verdict: rework it. Do not scrap it.**

Under this framing your AI content pipeline stops being an awkward shortcut and becomes a Headcount Zero artifact. "I automated my own LinkedIn presence for six months, here are the real numbers, here is what 500k impressions were actually worth" is a strong post and it converts your biggest liability into your first proof point.

Three changes:

1. **Demote it from publish engine to draft engine.** It finds and drafts. You add a first-person take and a number from your own work. Never auto-publish.
2. **Change the subject matter.** Stop aggregating generic engineering news. Post about your own operation, through the Headcount Zero lens.
3. **Cut volume if quality demands it.** Three strong posts beat seven generic ones, because per-post engagement velocity drives distribution.

If the reworked posts do not beat the old ones on saves and comments within 30 days, go fully manual on two flagship posts per week.

## 5. Apps, audience, and money

### The mismatch, and why it stops mattering

Your audience will be engineers. Interviewium targets interview candidates and Wait, Professor targets learners. Engineers are not the buyers for either. In the previous framing this was the central risk.

Under Headcount Zero it is not a risk, because the apps are not what you sell to the audience. They are the lab. They generate the numbers, the failures, and the case studies that the content is about.

That splits cleanly into two funnels:

**Apps to consumers:** ASO, learner and interview-prep communities, short-form video demos, creators who already reach students. None of this touches your dev audience. The DSA app is a partial exception, since some engineers are themselves interviewing, but do not build the plan around that.

**Brand to audience:** grows followers now (priority 1), builds career optionality (priority 3), generates consulting inbound (priority 4). Later it monetizes on what this audience actually wants, which is the agent systems you built to run the company. That is a real product with genuine audience-product fit, and it is a much better second act than trying to push consumer apps at engineers.

### Learning to sell, as an engineer

Sell the way engineers buy: specificity, proof, honest tradeoffs, no hype. Your benchmarks-and-receipts instinct is already a sales style, you just have not pointed it at your own products.

Do the first selling yourself and do not delegate it early. Read a little on positioning and funnels, then learn by shipping offers and measuring conversion. Treat objections as bug reports.

## 6. The 90-day plan

### Days 1 to 30: build before you brand

The framing dies instantly if the posts are speculative. Before announcing anything, pick three business functions and actually build the systems.

- Build 3 Replaced a Hire systems with real cost and outcome data.
- Rewrite LinkedIn headline and X bio around Headcount Zero. Retire Impact Engineer.
- Rework the LinkedIn pipeline to draft-plus-opinion. Ship 3 to 4 posts per week.
- Start X: 1 thread per week, 25 substantive replies per week.
- Create Telegram and Substack shells. Do not launch them yet.

**30-day success:** three shipped systems with numbers. Pipeline reworked. LinkedIn comment quality visibly up. First X reply that out-reaches your own posts.

### Days 31 to 60: launch the owned channels

- Launch Telegram in Russian. Cross-promote from LinkedIn, X, and the local tech community. 2 to 3 posts per week.
- Launch Substack free tier. First monthly essay from the month's artifacts. Turn on Recommendations, arrange 1 or 2 swaps.
- Double down on whichever X format produced your breakout.
- Start the consumer funnel for the DSA app: ASO pass, one short-form demo. Keep it separate from the brand.

**60-day success:** Telegram in the low hundreds. Substack seeded from existing subscribers. One post that clearly outperformed everything before it. App ASO live.

### Days 61 to 90: prove the lens

- Publish the signature piece. Something like "Six months, zero employees, here is every system I built and what each one cost." This is the thing you want people to link to when they explain who you are.
- Pitch one podcast or conference talk on running a zero-headcount operation.
- Choose the monetization path and set up the first offer. Most likely the agent systems, not the apps.

**90-day success:** measurable follower growth across LinkedIn and X. Someone repeats your framing back to you unprompted. All four channels live. One signature artifact with outsized reach. App downloads showing signal independent of the brand.

### What to defer

Paid Substack tier. Heavy app marketing spend. Any consulting push. All of it waits until the engine has proven reach.

### The one non-negotiable

One real artifact per week. If Speechify workload makes that impossible, cut a platform before you cut the artifact. The artifact is the entire engine and everything else is distribution.

## 7. Caveats

- Platform algorithm specifics come largely from consultant and vendor analysis rather than primary documentation. Treat exact percentages as directional. The directions themselves (authenticity rewarded, links penalized, dwell time matters, replies drive X discovery) are corroborated widely.
- Indie app revenue outcomes are highly variable and mostly disappointing. This plan optimizes for audience and optionality first, which matches your stated ranking and is also the more reliable outcome.
- The 90-day targets are calibrated to your current trajectory, not guaranteed. Adjust if the day job spikes.
- Headcount Zero has a shelf life if you ever hire. That is fine. Brands are allowed a second act, and "the year I ran on zero headcount" is a strong origin story even after it stops being literally true.
