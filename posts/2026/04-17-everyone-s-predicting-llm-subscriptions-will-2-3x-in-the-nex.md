Everyone’s predicting LLM subscriptions will 2-3x in the next two years. I keep reading these takes and nodding halfway, then disagreeing.

A few things don’t add up for me.

Market competition is fierce. Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI, Chinese labs, and open-weight models are all fighting for the same devs. Doubling prices while your competitor holds the line is how you lose your userbase in a quarter. Sure, a clear winner could emerge and pricing power follows, but we’re nowhere near that.

Small models are catching up fast. Haiku 4.5 doing most of what Opus 4 did a year ago is not a fluke. Smarter routing (cheap model for 80% of turns, expensive model for the hard 20%) is already cutting real costs. The labs have every incentive to push this because it improves their margins without raising your bill.

Inference costs keep dropping. New hardware, better kernels, speculative decoding, distillation. The per-token cost curve has been going down steeply and consistently. Prices at the top can stay flat while the thing underneath gets cheaper.

Then there’s the replacement argument. If teams have already restructured around Claude Code or Codex doing the work of a mid-level engineer, a 2-3x price hike doesn’t send those jobs back. It just shifts spend: companies eat the cost, or they route more through cheaper models, or they self-host. The org chart doesn’t un-flatten because your seat went from $200 to $500.

The more interesting question to me is what happens to the bottom of the market. Indie devs, students, hobbyists. That’s where a price jump actually bites, and also where a lot of the ecosystem momentum comes from.

Curious what others are seeing. Are you budgeting for higher AI spend next year, or betting on the cheap tier getting good enough?
