It only knows the world up to 1930. No internet, no WWII, no moon landing. You can chat with it like a person stuck in that decade.

That's a wild artifact on its own. But it made me think about school.

What if every history class had a vintage model per period. One trained on pre-1789 text for the French Revolution. One on early 20th century for the World Wars. Students could ask questions and get answers shaped by what people actually believed at the time, not a modern summary of it.

A textbook gives you the polished narrative. A vintage model gives you the contemporary worldview, biases included. Those are very different things, and the second one is almost impossible to get from a book unless you already know which primary sources to dig up.

The tradeoff is real though. With a book you know the author, the bias, the year. With a model trained on hundreds of billions of tokens, you have no idea what specific source shaped any given answer. You can't even know what you're missing.

Still, it feels like a more interesting use of a 13B model than another generic chatbot.
