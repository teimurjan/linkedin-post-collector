Google is sunsetting Gemini CLI and Gemini Code Assist on June 18, and the replacement is being tested in the feedback loop.

Antigravity 2.0 shipped at I/O on May 20. Three Google products fold into it: Gemini CLI, Gemini Code Assist, and AI Studio. Free users and AI Pro/Ultra subscribers lose access to the old extensions on June 18. New GitHub org installs of Code Assist stop the same day. Google's own framing is that they wanted "a single product built for today's multi-agent reality."

The migration kicker is in the fine print. Google has already said there is no one-to-one feature parity at launch. So enterprise teams that picked Code Assist for specific workflows are being routed to a strict subset, on someone else's deadline.

The Pragmatic Engineer headline calls it "Antigravity 2.0 takes 'IDE' out of its new IDE." Early users report bugs, poor UX, and a token burn rate they didn't budget for. That's the product Google is asking everyone to migrate to in four weeks.

When a vendor folds three of your tools into one agent platform within 12 months, your IDE choice stops being yours. The lock-in moved from the editor to the harness. The new question isn't "what editor do you use," it's "which agent platform owns your repo, and how hard is the exit."

Cursor, Claude Code, and Codex CLI are walking toward the same shape from different starts. Antigravity is the first time someone in this race forcibly retired the older tools to get there.

Forcing a paying customer base onto a product that its own users call broken is a tell. The editor isn't what Google thinks it's selling. The agent harness is.
