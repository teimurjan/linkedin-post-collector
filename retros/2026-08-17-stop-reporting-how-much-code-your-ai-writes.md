# Retro — Stop reporting how much code your AI writes

**Impressions: 749**, against a 2-to-7-day scrape-age cohort median of **571** (n=4). Scraped ~4 days after posting, so this is compared to the cohort of its own age, not the pooled corpus. It beat that cohort by 1.3x. Likes (4) and shares (2) scraped; comments did not.

## Tier and room size

The subject, read strictly, is a single vendor's self-reported number: Anthropic disclosed a 46% merge rate on its own unsupervised Claude Code deployment. That is textbook t0 — one vendor's own metric — and t0 subjects in the corpus land 76–236. This one landed at 749, roughly 3x above t0's observed ceiling, in the empty gap below t1's floor (1271).

That overshoot is the interesting part, and it is not "heat." The `heat is not size` rule on `wiki/audience.md` warns that topical volume predicts nothing, and the auto-mode miss proved it. But this post did not just ride volume. The wedge reframed a discourse the audience is already *in*: "AI writes 86% of our code" is a claim builders repeat and argue about constantly. The standing audience is around that framing, not around Anthropic's specific number — so the vendor metric borrowed a larger room than a vendor metric owns on its own. That is a size argument, not a heat argument: the room for the "generation % is a vanity metric" debate exists before the post.

## Hook and craft

Hook ("Stop reporting how much code your AI writes") is exact to the body — the whole post is that argument, delivered through the generation-vs-merge-rate contrast and the unmeasured cost of the 208 rejected PRs. No hook inflation. Craft was clean.

## Discussion

No comment data scraped, so the intended angle (reviewers, not generators, are the bottleneck) is unvalidated. Recorded `null`, not inferred from silence.

## Decision: repeat

Repeat the move, with the ceiling understood. The thing to repeat is **anchoring a t0 artifact to a framing the whole audience already argues about** — that is what lifted a single-vendor number above its tier band and past its cohort. The thing to keep honest: this is still a t0 subject, and 749 is a modest beat, not a t1/t2 breakout. Do not expect a vendor metric to reach thousands; expect it to clear its band when the wedge rents a bigger room.
